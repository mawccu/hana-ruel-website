Step 1 of 6Stretch

  1. Process

    Six steps. Two to six weeks.

    Oil paint dries slowly, and that's the point. Scroll to watch a painting come together.

  2. Step 1

    Stretch the linen.

    Raw Belgian linen goes over kiln-dried pine bars, stapled every 5 cm and pulled tight like a drum.

    3.8 cm stretcher · 390 g/m² linen
  3. Step 2

    Prime it.

    Three coats of gesso, sanded between each, so the oil sits on the surface and never touches the fibres.

    3 coats · 24 hours between
  4. Step 3

    Wash and sketch.

    A thin wash of burnt sienna takes the glare off the white. The composition goes on in charcoal.

    Burnt sienna · vine charcoal
  5. Step 4

    Block in the big shapes.

    Sky, sea, sun. Big brushes, thin paint. Each layer carries a little more oil than the one below it. Painters call this fat over lean.

    Flat 14 · lean mixture
  6. Step 5

    Layers and light.

    The slow part. Reflections, the boat, the last glints on the water. Days go by between sessions.

    Round 4 · 2–5 weeks
  7. Step 6

    Varnish and sign.

    Once it's dry to the touch it gets retouch varnish and a signature, then goes into a crate with its certificate.

    Retouch varnish · signed on the back

The studio

Hi, I'm Hana.

I paint in a small room above a bakery in Lisbon, which means everything I make smells faintly of bread for the first week. I work on four or five canvases at once and finish about thirty a year.

The studio is open to visitors on the first Saturday of every month. Come say hi.
